## 7. Execution-time drift analysis (check b) — researcher's turnover warning corrected

**Finding: the U5 claim that price drift "could push the batch against the turnover guard and shave
the last order" does not hold under this codebase.** Verified mechanics:

1. The turnover guard uses **limit-price notional** (risk.py:84, 176–183), and the batch total at
   limits is 49,315.30 ≤ 50,000 — all four orders fit **now**, at plan time. There is no
   execution-time re-sizing: `execute` submits each approved order once, at the fixed planned
   quantity and limit (cli.py:484–514). Nothing "shaves" the 4th order at execution.
2. Buy limit fills can only occur at prices **≤ limit**, so realized fill turnover ≤ 49,315.30 =
   49.32% < 50% **by construction**. Upward price drift causes **non-fill** (passive limit), not
   higher cost; downward drift lowers cost and frees cash. The 0.685pp headroom is against the
   worst-case (all-fill-at-limit) case, which is already the ceiling.
3. **The real drift risk is the pre-flight deviation check, and it is batch-atomic:** if any
   symbol's fresh last price moves >3% from its fixed limit, `execute` rejects the **entire batch**,
   nothing submitted (cli.py:441–461). Approximate trip bands (last price): SPY < 711.82 or
   > 755.85; EFA < 98.50 or > 104.59; VNQ < 92.21 or > 97.92; DBC < 29.34 or > 31.15. An Iran-gap
   in DBC or a hawkish shock could trip one symbol and block all four. That is conservative and
   correct — **do not override it; re-plan through the kernel** if it fires (C2).
4. Execute does not re-evaluate portfolio stateful guards (cash/gross/turnover) against live
   prices — acceptable here because fixed-limit buys have the plan-time numbers as their worst
   case, and all of those pass with wide margins (cash 50.68% vs 5% floor). Noted for the record.
5. Partial-fill outcome is acceptable: unfilled SPY/DBC leave more cash; the kernel re-targets at
   the next plan. No top-ups or re-submissions inside the FOMC window (C2).
